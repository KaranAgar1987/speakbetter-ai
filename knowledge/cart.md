---

## 1. Framework at a glance

**CART** = **C**ontext → **A**dversity → **R**esolution → **T**akeaway.

A four-step structure that, in verified sources, “works for almost any story you want to tell” (SRC-002), with emphasis on important moments and cutting unnecessary detail (SRC-001).

**Length guidance (not a rule):** SRC-001 suggests that most stories **at work** are *ideally* about **1–3 minutes**—use that as coaching guidance, not a requirement to pass or “validate” a story.

**Aliases in sources:** C.A.R.T., CART, “Context, Adversity, Resolution, Takeaway.”

---

## 2. Key cards (quick reference)

### English

| Card | One line | Coach prompt |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **C — Context** | Where, when, who, and what they want—briefly. | “Set the scene in one or two sentences. Who is this about and what did they want?” |
| **A — Adversity** | Challenges, conflicting priorities, uncertainty, tension—and how it felt. | “What was hard or unclear? What was at stake emotionally? Where did the pressure show up?” |
| **R — Resolution** | What they did and how it turned out. | “What action or decision changed things? What happened in the end?” |
| **T — Takeaway** | What you learned—one or two sentences, not a lecture. | “Finish with what *you* learned, not what the listener ‘should’ do.” |

### Hindi (key cards — coach-facing; spoken, professional)

| Card | एक लाइन में | कोच प्रॉम्प्ट |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **C — Context** | जगह, समय, कौन, और उनकी ज़रूरत—छोटा सीन। | “एक-दो वाक्य में—यह किसके बारे में है, और उन्हें क्या चाहिए था?” |
| **A — Adversity** | चुनौती, टकराती जिम्मेदारियाँ, असमंजस, तनाव—और एहसास। | “कहाँ अटका? क्या दाँव पर था—अंदर कैसा लग रहा था?” |
| **R — Resolution** | उन्होंने क्या किया, स्थिति कैसे बदली। | “कौन सा कदम उठाया—अंत में क्या हुआ?” |
| **T — Takeaway** | आपकी सीख—छोटी, व्यक्तिगत। | “‘मैंने सीखा कि…’ से समेटें—सुनने वाले को नसीहत न दें।” |

## 3. Verified step definitions (for coach logic)

Wording aligned with SRC-001 and SRC-002.

### Step 1 — Context

- Set the scene: **where** and **when**, **who** the main character is, **what they want**.
- Keep it **short** so you can move quickly into adversity (SRC-001).

### Step 2 — Adversity

Treat adversity as more than “something broke.” In coaching, include when relevant:

- **Challenges** (tough decisions, relationship friction, obstacles—SRC-001)
- **Conflicting priorities** (two things that both matter but pull apart)
- **Uncertainty** (not knowing outcome, role, or right move)
- **Tension** (SRC-002: where things go wrong and tension builds)
- **Emotions** (what’s at stake for the character; how they feel—SRC-001)

SRC-001 calls this the **longest and most crucial** part: struggle, stakes, and feelings.

### Step 3 — Resolution

- How the character **responds**; **actions or decisions**; **what happens** and how they change (SRC-001, SRC-002).

### Step 4 — Takeaway

- What **you learned**; the point in **one or two sentences**; connect to the audience (SRC-001).
- **Verified phrasing preference:** Frame as *“What I learned from that experience”* rather than *“You should take away…”* (SRC-001).

## 4. Coach behavior rules

| Do | Don’t |
|----|--------|
| Check that all four steps are present (even briefly). | Invent quotes or attribute SpeakBetter illustrations to Philipp Humm. |
| Nudge shorter context if setup runs long. | Treat 1–3 minutes as a mandatory cap or validation gate. |
| Draw out priorities, uncertainty, tension, and emotion in **Adversity**. | Use takeaway as a moralizing lecture. |
| Offer 1–3 minutes as **guidance** for most work stories (SRC-001). | Present §7 internal coaching notes as a user-facing “score” or pass/fail. |

**Optional (SRC-002, single-source on same page):** User may say “experience,” “example,” or “case” instead of announcing “a story.”

## 5. Examples

### 5.1 Source example — dentist / change (SRC-001, verified)

**Status:** Step labels and story content **verified** against [SRC-001](https://storylab.co/structure/) (April 22, 2024). Below: structure matches the article; wording is **condensed paraphrase**—for exact quotes, use the extract file.

| Step | Verified content (condensed paraphrase from SRC-001) |
|------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| **C** | April 2022, dentist in Amsterdam for a **routine check-up**; dentist suggests adding **1mm** of filling to his front teeth; years of grinding had made them very small; it sounded like a good idea. |
| **A** | **About 30 minutes later**, after the procedure, he lisps when asked how his new teeth feel; **the first day** they feel completely alien—lisping, teeth clashing, even spitting; he worries he will need a new job, that no one will invite him to speak, and that he may return to banking and Excel. |
| **R** | **On the second day** it still feels weird but a little less so; he hates it but reminds himself to be patient; **one week later** it is completely fine and he hardly notices the filling; teeth are still a bit short and gray, but he is pretty happy with them. |
| **T** | That is what change is: at first it feels a little odd, maybe you will not like it, but **after a few rounds** it will feel more natural. |

**Coach note:** Humm frames this for workshops on **embracing change**—not a workplace job story, but a full CART arc from a Tier-1 source.

### 5.2 Original workplace illustrations (SpeakBetter AI)

**Label:** These are **original SpeakBetter AI illustrations** for practice—not from Philipp Humm or research extracts.

#### Illustration A — Sprint demo missed (engineering lead)

| Step | Text |
|------|------|
| **C** | “Last Tuesday, ten minutes before our client demo, I was in the war room with my PM—we needed the payment flow live.” |
| **A** | “Staging was down, the backup env was on an old branch, and I could see the client’s exec on the call already waiting.” |
| **R** | “I walked them through a recorded clip for the broken path, shipped a hotfix in parallel, and sent a follow-up link that night.” |
| **T** | “What I learned is that an honest partial demo plus a clear recovery plan beats pretending nothing failed.” |

#### Illustration B — Difficult feedback (new manager)

| Step | Text |
|------|------|
| **C** | “In my first month as team lead, I had a 1:1 with Alex, who’d missed deadlines three sprints in a row.” |
| **A** | “I was torn between being fair and being firm—afraid they’d shut down, and unsure if my notes were too harsh.” |
| **R** | “I named the pattern, asked what was blocking them, and we agreed on one smaller commitment for the next week.” |
| **T** | “What I learned is that specific examples and curiosity land better than a vague ‘you need to improve.’” |

#### Illustration C — Sales call (account executive)

| Step | Text |
|------|------|
| **C** | “On a renewal call in Q3, our champion had gone quiet and finance joined without warning.” |
| **A** | “They said they were re-evaluating vendors; my deck had last year’s pricing and I didn’t know who in the room actually owned the decision.” |
| **R** | “I paused, asked what success would look like in twelve months, and offered a pilot scoped to one team.” |
| **T** | “What I learned is that when the room shifts, questions beat sliding another slide.” |

#### Illustration D — Interview rejection (job seeker)

| Step | Text |
|------|------|
| **C** | “Last month I finished a final-round interview for a product role I’d been preparing for all quarter.” |
| **A** | “They rejected me; the recruiter said I was strong overall but the other finalist had more domain depth. I felt embarrassed updating friends, and I kept replaying whether my answers were too technical and not enough about why I wanted *this* team.” |
| **R** | “I sat with that feedback, emailed the recruiter to ask for one concrete gap to work on, then changed how I prepared: I rewrote two portfolio stories with clearer outcomes, and booked three mock interviews where I practiced ‘why this role’ and motivation—not only technical drills.” |
| **T** | “What I learned is that rejection only becomes useful when I name one change in how I prepare next time—not when I treat it as proof I don’t belong.” |

## 6. Common gaps (coach diagnostics)

| Symptom | Likely missing step | Coach move |
|---------|---------------------|------------|
| Timeline with no tension | **Adversity** | “What was uncertain or conflicting? How did you feel under that pressure?” |
| Jumps straight to lesson | **Context** or **Resolution** | “Where were you? What did you actually do?” |
| Ends with “So you all should…” | **Takeaway** style | Reframe to “What I learned…” |
| Long background, no punch | **Context** too long | “Shorten setup—then name the adversity.” |

## 7. Internal coaching notes (not a validation scale)

**For coach implementation only.** Do **not** show users a pass/fail badge, numeric grade, or “validated story” status.

Optional rehearsal prompts if a step feels thin:

| Step | If it feels missing or flat, ask… |
|------|-----------------------------------|
| **C** | “Who and what did they want—in one breath?” |
| **A** | “Where were priorities in conflict? What was unclear? What emotion showed up?” |
| **R** | “What did they *do*—one concrete move?” |
| **T** | “Can you end with one sentence of what *you* learned?” |

This is a **coaching checklist**, not a rubric that certifies story quality.
